# Volatility-Adaptive Crypto and FX Execution Engine

## Why this project exists

I started this project, quite naively, because I wanted to see results and proof of the concepts I have been studying in my journey of learning quantitative trading. One of the main inspirations is Option Volatility and Pricing by Sheldon Natenberg, known to be one of the entry level reads for new quants. I, however, want to apply some of these theories in practice. Options are priced off volatility, and long straddles and strangles buy volatility rather than betting on direction. So, on the topic of volatility, I did some more reading on the current quant models that are being used in practice, which led me to models that help forecast volatility.

I also unfortunately learnt, option prices already contain the market forecast of volatility as implied volatility, and a GARCH model aims to forecast the same thing. But theoretically, if my forecast is higher than the market forecast by enough to cover costs, then buying volatility should have a positive edge. If it is not higher, then there is no edge.

## The stages

Stage -1 asks whether the edge exists at all. It pulls BTC and ETH option prices from Deribit and takes implied vol from mark IV and the DVOL history. It compares implied vol with the GARCH forecast over the same horizon, at seven and thirty days to match weekly and monthly expiries, and it measures realised vol over those horizons from the five-minute bars. It reprices a long straddle and a long strangle at forecast vol and tests whether buying vol on the days the forecast sits above implied vol pays after costs, holding the trade to expiry.

Stage 0 is the offline model validation. It has three parts. Stage 0A sets the baseline, testing EWMA against GARCH(1,1) on daily realised variance. Each day has two numbers, the daily log return and the daily realised variance, which is the sum of the squared five-minute log returns inside that day. The models run at daily frequency and the five-minute bars are only the measuring tape. The split is chronological, 845 train days and 212 test days. The GARCH refits every twenty test days on an expanding window and the variance recursion is stepped daily, so the forecast moves every day between refits. Forecasts are scored on QLIKE with MSE, Mincer-Zarnowitz calibration and a Diebold-Mariano test, and there is no lookahead anywhere. Stage 0B tests asymmetric and fat-tailed GARCH. It fits GJR-GARCH and EGARCH with Student-t and skew-t errors, compares the fits on AIC, BIC and log-likelihood, and backtests 95 percent and 99 percent Value-at-Risk bands with Kupiec and Christoffersen tests for coverage and for clustering of exceedances. Stage 0C tests HAR models on intraday realised volatility. It aggregates five-minute returns into daily, weekly and monthly realised vol components and separates continuous diffusion from jumps, then benchmarks the QLIKE and MSE improvement against the best Stage 0B model with a Diebold-Mariano test and splits the evaluation across high-vol days and calm regimes. Its exit artifact is selected\_model.json, the frozen model specification the production engine implements.

Stages 1 to 5 are the live system, one module per stage. Stage 1 is multi-venue ingestion and deterministic replay. Async WebSocket connectors pull live ticks from the crypto and FX venues and normalise them into one canonical tick schema, a local limit order book tracker holds top of book prices, micro-price and imbalance, and a historical replayer reads Parquet tick archives and injects events through the same queue with virtual timestamps. Stage 2 is the streaming feature and volatility engine. It builds rolling five-minute return bars and intraday realised variance accumulators tick by tick and runs the winning model specification from Stage 0C compiled with Numba, alongside rolling bid-ask spread and cross-asset correlation. Stage 3 is the signal and volatility-targeted sizing. It computes a directional signal normalised by current intraday vol, sizes positions as target risk divided by forecast vol, and gates orders on spread and cost. Stage 4 is the pre-trade risk gateway. It enforces hard controls on maximum order notional, maximum net exposure, a drawdown kill switch and a stale-tick circuit breaker. Stage 5 is execution and telemetry. It simulates fills with realistic slippage and partial fills, runs a fault-injection suite covering flash crashes and dropped packets, and checks that a live paper session and its Parquet replay produce identical order logs.

## How the repo is organised

The repo is organised as one folder per stage. Each folder holds its own code, so a stage can be read and run on its own.
